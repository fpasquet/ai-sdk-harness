First release: sessions for AI SDK harness agents. `createSessionManager()` runs the conversations of your users with any `HarnessAgent` — Claude Code, Codex or another runtime — through their whole lifecycle: `create()` opens a session in the background, `send()` streams a turn in reply to a message, one turn at a time, and the session keeps its conversation, readable with `get()` and `list()`. A turn that pauses on a tool approval, or on a tool your application runs, leaves the session `awaiting-input` until `continue()` brings the answers, which it can read from the message `useChat` sends back. A session idle for `idleTimeoutMs` is suspended, its harness session stopped with its state and its sandbox freed, and resumed where it was on the next message; `shutdown()` suspends every session when your server stops, so the next start resumes them, and a session a crash left live is marked `interrupted`. `subscribe()` sends every change, for a list that follows the sessions live.

Where each session runs is up to `sharedSandbox()`, which gives every session a view of one sandbox with a port of its own for its bridge (`fork()` of `ai-sdk-sandbox-sbx` and `ai-sdk-sandbox-cloud-run`), suspending the session idle the longest when every port is taken and stopping the sandbox once no session holds it with `stopWhenUnused`, or `sandboxPerSession()`, which gives each one a sandbox of its own. Sessions are kept in a `SessionStore`: `createMemorySessionStore()` by default, or yours on your database. `SessionNotFoundError`, `SessionConflictError` and `SessionCapacityError` say what went wrong, with an `isInstance()` that works across copies of the package.
